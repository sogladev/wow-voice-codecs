This page plays the same sentence through that codec and through Opus at several rates and packet sizes, decoded exactly as the client would, so you can hear the difference instead of reading about it.

What the numbers say

PESQ is ITU-T P.862 on a 1.0–4.5 scale, higher is better; it is a telephony measure, so treat it as a ranking rather than a promise of how a raid sounds. Wire bitrate includes the client's 7-byte voice header plus UDP and IPv4 overhead.

How this was measured

  • The 3.3.5a codec is the decompiled ComSat/SPIRIT source, compiled standalone and driven the way ComSatSoundCODEC.cpp drives it: five 20 ms subframes per 100 ms frame on the way in, and while (bits - consumed >= 72) on the way out.
  • Opus is libopus 1.6 in VoIP mode, one encoder and one decoder per stream, with the frame sizes and packet layouts shown in the table.
  • Speech is the Open Speech Repository American English set, published for VoIP codec testing, at 8 kHz.
  • PESQ (P.862) and STOI are computed against the uncompressed source. Loss runs drop every 20th packet; Opus runs use in-band FEC and decode the gap from the next packet.
  • Each clip is normalised to −20 LUFS before encoding to MP3 so codec gain differences do not bias the comparison. MP3 is far above every candidate's own bitrate, but it is still a lossy layer: the WAV link next to each clip is the exact decoded output.

PESQ scores one clean signal against one degraded signal. It says nothing about four talkers at once, the game's mixing, ducking, or the sound of a real headset microphone in a room. The absolute scores here will be higher than what you hear in game; the ranking is what should carry over.
